Renewing your business permit in Pasig

What to prepare, the dates to watch, what being late can cost, and Pasig's official links, in one page.

Usually dueWednesday, January 20, 2027 Add the dates to my calendar
Where
Business Permits and Licensing Office (BPLO)
Official links

We couldn't confirm an online renewal link on the city's own website. Ask the Business Permits and Licensing Office (BPLO), or watch the city's official announcements in December.

What to prepare

Most of the work is the same everywhere. Pasig may ask for a little more, so check its list too. Start in December: the barangay clearance comes first, and offices get busy in January.

1

Barangay Business Clearance

From the barangay hall where the business is. You need this year's before the Mayor's Permit.

  • Last year's barangay clearance
  • Proof of addressLease contract, or the title or tax declaration if you own the place.
  • Community Tax Certificate (cedula), if your barangay asks
  • Pay the barangay fee and keep the receipt
2

Mayor's Permit (business permit)

From Pasig's Business Permits and Licensing Office (BPLO), or online where the city offers it.

  1. Barangay Business Clearance for this year
  2. Last year's Mayor's Permit and its official receipt
  3. Last year's gross sales or receiptsUsually copies of your BIR returns, or a sworn declaration of gross sales.
  4. Community Tax Certificate (cedula) for the businessFrom the city or municipal treasurer.
  5. Proof of addressLease contract, or the title or tax declaration if you own the place.
  6. SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG proof of payment, if your city asksCommon for businesses with employees.
  7. Fire safety inspection (FSIC)Many cities have the fire inspection done as part of the renewal.
  8. Pay the assessed taxes and feesMany cities let you pay by quarter. Keep the official receipt.
  9. Get the new permit and display it at the business

What we know about Pasig

  • In 2025 the city extended the renewal deadline from January 20 to January 31.

    Source: Malaya Business Insight, January 2025 (opens in a new tab)

Extensions are announced by the city, often only around January 20 itself. Plan for January 20.

If you renew late

The Local Government Code lets a city add up to a 25% surcharge on what's unpaid, plus up to 2% interest a month (on the unpaid amount and the surcharge) for as long as it stays unpaid, up to 36 months. Cities may charge less, never more. Operating without a current permit can also get a business closed.
